## The Problem Isn't Belief. It's Execution.

International brands no longer need to be convinced that African markets matter. The opportunity is clear, and investment is following.

What often falls short is the way brands communicate once they arrive. Too many still communicate like tourists: a campaign designed at headquarters, translated, and dropped into markets that read it very differently. A press release sent to a generic media list. The same visuals and the same channels everywhere.

The result is a brand that is visible but not relevant.

That gap is why BOXCOM Africa exists, and why we are opening this site with an argument instead of a list of services.
:::

## What Twelve Years in Moroccan and African Media Taught Us

BOXCOM was founded in Casablanca in 2014 as a 360 degree agency covering video, digital and media relations. As demand for sub-Saharan operations grew, we separated the two activities so each could get the focus it deserved. BOXCOM Digital kept the digital clients and expertise. In 2024 we launched BOXCOM Africa to house our PR experts, our international clients, and our network of partner agencies and media across the continent.

Along the way we learned the same lesson in market after market: localization cannot be the last step of a campaign. At BOXCOM Africa it is part of the communication strategy from the beginning.

Our role is to bridge global brand strategies with local cultural, linguistic and media realities, so a brand's positioning can be expressed effectively in each market while its identity stays consistent.
:::

## What This Looks Like in Practice

The difference rarely shows up in headline campaigns. It shows up in ordinary ones.

When a Saudi digital solutions group exhibited at GITEX Africa in Marrakech, its announcements mattered to three audiences reading in three different languages. Run as one story in one language, it would have reached a fraction of them. Split across Arabic, French and English media, with the spokesperson in front of Moroccan television, the same trade show appearance produced coverage in thirty-nine different outlets.

When an IT services firm needed engineers for its operation in northern Morocco, the obvious move was a recruitment campaign. What worked was an employer brand story: employees explaining why they had stayed in the north instead of leaving for Casablanca or abroad. It ran across national business and technology media and brought in three hundred and fifty applications.

When a Tunisian airline opened a Casablanca route, the story was never the route. It was what three weekly flights mean for the families and businesses on either side of it. That framing is the reason it ran in forty outlets in French and Arabic instead of appearing once in a travel section.

None of these were expensive campaigns. What they had in common was a story built for the market rather than translated into it.
:::

## One Brand, Different Ways to Connect

Communicating locally does not mean changing who a brand is. It means understanding how that identity should be expressed so it remains meaningful to the people the brand wants to reach.

Brands do not need to communicate identically everywhere to remain consistent. They need to communicate in a way that makes sense in every market they enter.
:::
